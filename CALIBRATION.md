# Calibration report: sim vs real NBA data

The engine is calibrated against real Basketball-Reference data (through the 2025-26 season), not memory.

- **Data:** `sumitrodatta/bball-reference-datasets` (public GitHub repo, a Basketball-Reference scrape). Clone it next to this project.
- **Targets:** `python3 scripts/real-targets.py [path/to/Data]` computes everything below and writes `data/real-targets.json`.
- **Comparison:** `node scripts/real-compare.js 80 3` simulates 3 leagues x 80 seasons and prints real vs sim. `node scripts/ovr-equalize.js` checks that equal overall ratings give equal production across archetypes.
- **"perf"** is one production number used for both: `pts*(1+2.2*(TS%-0.57)) + 1.15*reb + 1.5*ast + 2*(stl+blk) - 1.1*tov`.

## What the real data changed

| Finding in the real data | What I had before | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Players aged 30+ lose scoring volume earlier than ratings suggest (25-ppg scorers: 25.0 at 28, 22.6 at 30, 20.1 at 34, 16.4 at 36) but ratings/production decline *flattens* after 33 | Scorers held 26+ ppg to 34; rating decline too steep at 34-36 | Age-based usage taper from 29; gentler rating decline from 31 |
| Only 5 seasons of 34+ ppg since 1980 (Jordan x2, Harden x2, Kobe); players who ever hit 30 average 2.35 such seasons, 52% reach 32+, 13% reach 34+ | Elite scorers had 4-6 thirty-point seasons | Planned 32-33 year (62% of elite scorers), rare 34-36 year (~12%), softer everyday cap |
| 7 triple-double seasons in history: Westbrook ages 28-32 (x4), Jokic 29-30 (x2), Oscar 23 | Window from 26; early assists capped too hard | Window starts 26-30 and rolls forward until the first one; early cap relaxed |
| Team wins: sd 12.5, year-to-year change 8.5, 5% of teams win 60+, 13% win <25; top-3 picks add +8 wins in year 1, +14 in year 2 | sd 8.7, change 4.8, almost no 60+ or sub-25 teams | Wider team spread and noise, tail shaping, weaker roster continuity, young-player impact |
| Production changes year to year with sd 4.8 perf | sd 2-3 | Shared season-level form shock |
| Stars play 80% of games (35 mpg, 30.7% usage, 61% TS) | 88% games, 36 mpg, 59% TS | More injuries and rest, minutes cap, TS tuned |
| P(All-Star) by draft slot: #1 73%, #2-3 48%, #4-5 35%, #6-10 20%, #11-14 9%, #15-20 9%, #21-30 5%, #31-45 3%, #46-60 2% | #1 95%, later picks ~0% | Noisier scouting with occasional big misses (sleepers) |
| Rookie lines by pick (#1: 16.7 ppg / 30.9 mpg ... #15-30: 6.7 / 18.0) | Lottery rookies 15% too good | Rookie minutes floors by slot |
| MVPs: age 26.5, 60 wins, 27.3 ppg, 31.7% usage; 27 distinct winners in 46 seasons; most is 5 (Jordan) | MVP ppg 30, usage 35%, 22 distinct winners | Voting balances production, wins and scoring; more noise and voter fatigue |
| The generational archetype produced 33% of the sim's top-24 players | Far too many all-rounders | Generational is rare (~1 per 4-5 classes); small per-archetype rating offsets |
| 246 players per season play 58+ games (the sim modelled 125); a real league has a deep rotation | Only each team's top 4-5 players existed | Every team now has a 4-6 player bench who play, progress, retire and compete for the core; every drafted rookie is kept (sleepers) |
| Per-36 lines of 119 real players (2025-26) vs the sim at their rating: defenders under-scored by 6 pts, dominators over-rebounded by 2.4, shooters under-rebounded by 1.7, steals ran low | Archetype templates were guesses | Templates retuned against real players (mean line error 0.93 -> 0.82); real rosters now rated from their actual production |

## Real players as a test set (2026-27 rosters rated from 2025-26 stats)

`src/real.js` is generated by `python3 scripts/build-real.py`. Each real player's rating is 65% their actual 2025-26 production (rank-mapped to the sim's talent curve, then aged forward with the real age curve) and 35% my earlier estimate. `node scripts/fit-real-archetypes.js` then asks, for every real player, what the engine predicts for their per-36 line at that rating and compares it with the real line:

## Known gaps (honest list)

- **Sustained stars:** 2.1 players per draft class with 5+ All-Star seasons (real 1.27); any All-Star per class 4.5 (real 5.35). Three-time All-Stars 2.9 (real 2.73).
- **Mid-tier production is 4-10% high** (ranks 10-150 perf; the 24th-best player is 40.4 vs 37.0), mostly assists, blocks and rebounds. Scoring by rank is within 3-10%. The top 3 players match.
- **30+ ppg players** (1.7 vs 2.1) and the top scorer (31.5 vs 32.6) run slightly low; ppg at ranks 35-150 is 5-12% high.
- **Year-to-year change** in team wins is 9.4 vs 8.5 and win persistence is 0.52 vs 0.60.
- **Archetype templates** still miss real defenders by about -1 point and -1 assist per 36, and real scorers/shooters by about +1 point per 36.
- **Triple-double rates** cannot be checked from season files; they rest on known career figures.
- **Salaries** are not calibrated (no salary file).
- **Real-roster ratings** blend 65% stats with 35% my prior, and some 2026 depth charts were conflicting preseason reports.

## Game-to-game spread (real play-by-play game logs, 2001-2024)
`scripts/real-gamelogs.py` rebuilds box scores from `shufinskiy/nba_data`; `scripts/gamelog-compare.js` feeds each real player-season average into `genGame`.
Points noise was too tight (sd/mean 0.234 vs real 0.333, 40+/50+ games 3-8x too rare for 18-28 ppg players). Noise raised to 0.29: sd/mean ≈ 0.31, 30+/40+/50+ rates within ~10-20% of real across ppg bins. Rebounds/assists spread already matched. Triple-double rate at the very top lines is still ~15% light.

**TD correlation:** rebounds and assists now share a per-game "engagement" factor (sd 0.13) on top of the minutes factor, so big boards and big dimes cluster like real triple-double nights. Overall TD rate for 59 real TD-capable seasons: 16.3% sim vs 17.8% real (was 15.1%); rebound/assist spread still ≈ real (0.461/0.523 vs 0.452/0.522).
